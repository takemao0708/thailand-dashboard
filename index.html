Key Empirical Findings & Risk Analysis
Group 4 Insights
1. Holiday Travel Seasonality Spikes

The 5-year aggregate uncovers massive nationwide fatality surges during major holidays: April (Songkran Festival: 13,406 crashes) and December (New Year Festival: 12,553 crashes). Combined, these two months account for nearly 25% of all annual road trauma.

2. The Volume vs. Lethality Paradox

While Bangkok and Nakhon Ratchasima lead in sheer accident count (11,372 and 4,882 crashes), southern and eastern transit provinces exhibit significantly deadlier outcomes per collision. Surat Thani is the deadliest province with 491 fatalities (18.3% severity), and Chanthaburi suffers an alarming 23.4% fatality rate.

3. Dangerous Road Geometry & Private Accesses

Although Straight Flat roads handle ~70% of accidents due to speeding, Private Property Access junctions and Uncontrolled Intersections pose extreme mortality rates approaching 30 deaths per 100 crashes due to right-angle T-bone collisions and poor line-of-sight.

4. Primary Triggers: Speeding & Sleep Deprivation

Excessive speeding constitutes over 71% of all recorded crash triggers (95,061 crashes). Driver fatigue ("Fell Asleep at Wheel") represents the second largest single trigger with 8,828 crashes, peaking during nocturnal hours between 02:00 - 05:00 and afternoon twilight.

Policy Roadmap & Data Scope
Actionable Interventions
Strategic Safety Interventions:
  • Regional Checkpoint Surge: Reallocate traffic police patrols and sobriety checkpoints to Highway corridors in Surat Thani, Chanthaburi, and Nakhon Ratchasima during festival weeks.
  • Geometric Junction Redesign: Mandate acceleration/deceleration lanes and rumble strips on rural highways intersecting private property and commercial accesses.
  • Automated Speed Enforcement: Deploy automated speed cameras along major straight highway stretches where over 70% of speed-induced rollovers occur.
  • Diurnal Lighting Audits: Install high-intensity solar street lighting and reflective markers along high-fatality rural curves between 18:00 and 04:00.
Dataset Limitations:

This dataset records road accidents administered by the Ministry of Transport (MOT), including Department of Highways (DOH), Department of Rural Roads (DRR), and Expressway Authority of Thailand (EXAT). It does not include minor intra-city municipal alleyways (Soi) or local tambon roads administered by local government organizations.
